- **Utility maximizing rule**
	- the consumer's money should be spent so that the marginal utility per dollar of each goods equal each other
	- MU of x / P of x = MU of y / P of y

## Elasticity
- **Elasticity of demand coefficient** = % change in Q / % change in P
- **Elastic Demand**: Quantity is sensitive to a change in price
	- many substitutes
	- luxuries
	- elasticity coefficient >1
- **Inelastic Demand**: Quantity is insensitive to a change in price
	- few substitutes
	- necessities
	- elasticity coefficient <1
- **Cross-price elasticity of demand** = % change in Q of a / % change in P of b
	- +: substitutes
	- -: complements
- **Income-elasticity of demand** = % change in Q / % change in income
	- +: normal good
	- -: inferior good
- **Total revenue test**
	- PED>1 (relatively inelastic): price ↑, TR ↑, price ↓, TR ↓
	- PED<1 (relatively elastic): price ↑, TR ↓, price ↓ , TR ↑
- **Price ceilings & floors**
	- price ceilings must be below equilibrium, price floors must be above equilibrium
	- they create deadweight loss

## Income & Substitution Effects
- **The substitution effect** of a change in the price of a good is the change in the quantity of that good demanded as the consumer substitutes the good that has become relatively cheaper for the good that has become relatively more expensive.
- **The income effect** of a change in the price of a good is the change in the quantity of that good demanded that results from a change in the consumer’s purchasing power when the price of the good changes.
**In most cases, the demand curve slopes downward is solely because of substitution effect. Income effect reinforces it.**

## Consumer & Producer Surplus
- Consumer surplus is the price one is willing to pay minus the price paid (or market price)
- Producer surplus is the money the producer sells for minus the money they were willing to sell for.  
- ![[Pasted image 20241110110147.png|400]]

**Markets**
- Markets maximize total surplus. **total surplus is maximized (no deadweight loss) when there's allocative efficiency**
- The people who “want” the product most will get it.
- The producers most willing to sell will get the sale.
- When both sides benefit, the sale happens
- When both sides don’t, the sale does not.

## Perfect competition
- Definition
	- many small firms
	- **identical** products
	- **low barriers to entry**
	- sellers has no need to advertise
	- sellers are **price-takers** (demand curve of one individual firm is horizontal)
- ==**Profit maximizing rule**: MR = MC== 
- **Shut down rule**: should shut down when price falls **below AVC**
  
- **Productive efficiency**:
	- producing at the lowest possible cost
	- graph: **P = min. ATC**
- **Allocative efficiency** ( AKA socially optimal):
	- producing at the amount most desirable by the society
	- graph: **P = MC**
- **Equilibrium**: normal profit (0 economic profit)

**Perfectly competitive firms are always allocatively efficient and achieve productive efficiency in the long run.**

     Because ==to get more customers (increase demand), producers need to decrease their price for all the units they sell== (assume there's no price discrimination). So revenue does not increase simply by the amount of price decrease. In other words, you make more money by selling more products, but you get less for each.

### Natural Monopoly
- **Definition**: situation where economies of scale (high fixed costs) make it so that a single company makes sense.
- ==ATC slopes downward== over the range of output relevant for market demand

**How to regulate a monopoly to maximize consumer benefit?**
- ==set price to MC (D=MC)== (unless MC is below ATC; if then, set ==D=ATC==)

	2. D=ATC: no economic profit

## Monopolistic Competition
- **Definition**
	1. many competing producers with **differentiated products**
	2. **free entry and exit in the long run**

![[Pasted image 20241112112620.png|300]]
- **At equilibrium**
	- P=ATC
	- Q: MR=MC
	- productively inefficient and allocatively inefficient
	- normal profit (0 economic profit)
- **Profit**
	- P> ATC
	- **Firms will enter**, so each individual firm has a smaller share of the market, so the **D and MR both shift to the left** until there is zero economic profit (P=ATC)
	- back to long run
- **Loss**
	- P< ATC
	- **Firms will exit, so D and MR both shift to the right**

## Public Goods
- Rival goods: a good that gets used up as it is consumed. *e.g. physical products*
- Non-rival goods: a good that is not diminished as it is consumed. *e.g. digital product or services such as music streaming services*
- Excludable goods: a good that can only be consumed by those who pay for it. *e.g. concert tickets*
- Non-excludable goods: A good that can be consumed by non-payers. *e.g. public firework display*
-  The free-rider problem: problem with non-excludable goods, people enjoy benefits of a good without paying for them
- **Public goods: both non-rival and non-excludable.** *e.g. national defense*

## Definitions of Profit
- Implicit cost: a type of opportunity cost, dollar value of forgone benefit
- Explicit cost: money actually paid
- usually opportunity cost is break into implicit cost and explicit cost

- Economic profit = revenue - (implicit cost + explicit cost)

- **Normal profit**
	- **0 economic profit**
	- **equilibrium in perfect competition and monopolistic competition**
