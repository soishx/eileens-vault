## Public Goods
- Rival goods: a good that gets used up as it is consumed. *e.g. physical products*
- Non-rival goods: a good that is not diminished as it is consumed. *e.g. digital product or services such as music streaming services*
- Excludable goods: a good that can only be consumed by those who pay for it. *e.g. concert tickets*
- Non-excludable goods: A good that can be consumed by non-payers. *e.g. public firework display*
-  The free-rider problem: problem with non-excludable goods, people enjoy benefits of a good without paying for them
- **Public goods: both non-rival and non-excludable.** *e.g. national defense*

### Categorizing Taxes
Classified by percentage of income as taxes
- **regressive taxes**
	- lower % for the rich
	- higher % for the poor
- **progressive taxes**
	- higher % for the rich
	- lower % for the poor
- **proportional taxes**
	- same for rich and poor
- **Shift on Lorenz Curve**
	- regressive: shift outward (less equal)
	- progressive: shift inward (more equal)
	- proportional: no shift
