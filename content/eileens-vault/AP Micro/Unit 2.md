## Elasticity
- **Elasticity of demand coefficient** = % change in Q / % change in P
- **Elastic Demand**: Quantity is sensitive to a change in price
	- many substitutes
	- luxuries
	- elasticity coefficient >1
- **Inelastic Demand**: Quantity is insensitive to a change in price
	- few substitutes
	- necessities
	- elasticity coefficient <1
- **Cross-price elasticity of demand** = % change in Q of a / % change in P of b
	- +: substitutes
	- -: complements
- **Income-elasticity of demand** = % change in Q / % change in income
	- +: normal good
	- -: inferior good
- **Total revenue test**
	- PED>1 (relatively inelastic): price ↑, TR ↑, price ↓, TR ↓
	- PED<1 (relatively elastic): price ↑, TR ↓, price ↓ , TR ↑
- **Price ceilings & floors**
	- price ceilings must be below equilibrium, price floors must be above equilibrium
	- they create deadweight loss

- **Utility maximizing rule** ^fc7353
	- the consumer's money should be spent so that the marginal utility per dollar of each goods equal each other
	- MU of x / P of x = MU of y / P of y

## Income & Substitution Effects
- **The substitution effect** of a change in the price of a good is the change in the quantity of that good demanded as the consumer substitutes the good that has become relatively cheaper for the good that has become relatively more expensive.
- **The income effect** of a change in the price of a good is the change in the quantity of that good demanded that results from a change in the consumer’s purchasing power when the price of the good changes.
**In most cases, the demand curve slopes downward is solely because of substitution effect. Income effect reinforces it.**

## Consumer & Producer Surplus
- Consumer surplus is the price one is willing to pay minus the price paid (or market price)
- Producer surplus is the money the producer sells for minus the money they were willing to sell for.  
- ![[Pasted image 20241110110147.png|400]]

**Markets**
- Markets maximize total surplus. **total surplus is maximized (no deadweight loss) when there's allocative efficiency**
- The people who “want” the product most will get it.
- The producers most willing to sell will get the sale.
- When both sides benefit, the sale happens
- When both sides don’t, the sale does not.
