## Labor Supply and Demand

**Market demand for labor**
![[Pasted image 20241214150157.png]]
- MRP (marginal revenue product) = MR x MP
- Shifts
	- demand of labor comes from
		1. product price
		2. product demand
		3. productivity of workers
	- If any of the factors increase MRP, D shifts right
	- If any of the factors decrease MRP, D shifts left

**Market supply for labor**

![[Pasted image 20241214150403.png]]
- Shifts
	- factors: number of workers, availability, population age, value of leisure, etc.

**Perfectly competitive factor market firms**
![[Pasted image 20241228162757.png|400]]

MRC: marginal resource cost
- Shifts
	- ![[Pasted image 20241228163717.png|400]]
	- Change in market cause change in firm:
		- **MRP of the last worker increased** cuz MRC and MRP intersects at a higher point
		- wage up
	- ![[Pasted image 20241228163838.png|400]]
	- Change only in an individual firm:
		- **MRP of the last worker stays the same** 
		- **Q of workers increase**

## Monopsony
- a single buyer of labor
-  ![[Pasted image 20241228173120.png|250]]
- Profit-maximize: MRP = MRC, but the wage is at the supply level
- Monopsonies **pays lower wages, and hires fewer workers** than perfectly competitive market
- **not allocatively efficient**
- DWL is the triangle between Qm & Qc
- Why MRC is higher than S?
  Because ==to increase the number of workers, the firm has to increase the wage and pay the same wage for every worker.==

## Least Cost Combinations
- least cost: marginal product of **labor** / price = marginal product of **capital** / price
- if not equal, want **more of the one with the higher MP/$**
