In the twentieth century Communist movements strongly affects women's struggle for rights. Those who supported women's rights movement encouraged education and equal work rights. 
Some communist governments or government members were concerned with women's rights and took active measures to ensure equal social rights, especially work rights. Document 1 demonstrates that Alexandra Kollontai, as a female Russian communist, realized that her party had neglected women's liberation and calling for women's rights due to the inspiration of strong bourgeois women's movement. To spread communism and to strengthen people's understanding of and support for the communist party, she decided to work for women's liberation and rights. As a member of the Bolshevik government, she might have been able to implement law reforms and spread the idea that women are equal with men. Thus, communists supported women's rights movement for their own needs of spreading communism. In comparison, Communist North Vietnamese Constitution of 1960 more directly addressed women's working rights and reproductive rights. Women enjoyed equal pay with men, fully paid maternity leave, and state-protected maternity hospitals. The way that the state directly wrote these measures that were essential to women's rights into the constitution, the supreme law that everyone must obey, demonstrates that communist movements strongly supported women's rights movement.
However, there were also situations where communists had a negative effect on women's rights movement.
