## Labor Union
organizations of workers that advocated for the right to bargain with employers and put the resulting agreements in a contract

- Unions improved workers’ lives by winning minimum wage laws, limits on the number of hours worked, overtime pay, and the establishment of a five-day work week.
- movements for voting right among working class: These reforms laid the foundation for expansion of the franchise (right to vote) to all men in 1918.
- banned child labor by law in 1843

## Intellectual Reaction
- utilitarianism: sought “the greatest good for the greatest number of people.”

A British philosopher, John Stuart Mill (1806–1873) criticized laissez-faire capitalism as inhumane to workers. He championed legal reforms to allow labor unions, limit child labor, and ensure safe working conditions in factories. While his ideas were controversial in his time, many of them eventually become widely adopted in industrial societies.

- Karl Marx (1818–1883) was a German scholar and writer who argued for socialism.
## Reforms
In the 19th century, the Ottoman Empire, China, and Japan each responded differently to industrialization. military, legal, educational, economic.
- e.g. China
	- China’s Self-Strengthening Movement sought military and economic modernization, but opposition from conservative figures like Empress Dowager Cixi slowed progress. After China’s defeat in the Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895), reform efforts intensified, but internal resistance and foreign intervention weakened China’s sovereignty.

## Society Changes
New social classes emerged:

- **Working class:** Factory and mine laborers with low wages and poor conditions.
- **Middle class:** Office managers, business owners, and professionals who gained wealth and influence.
- **Industrialists:** Wealthy factory owners who became more powerful than the aristocracy.

- **Women’s Roles:**
    - Working-class women labored in factories and coal mines for lower wages than men.
    - Middle-class women were expected to stay home, reinforcing the "cult of domesticity."
    - Feminist movements gained momentum, with events like the 1848 Seneca Falls Convention.

Environmental Consequences: Factories polluted air and water with coal emissions and industrial waste, leading to widespread health issues.
