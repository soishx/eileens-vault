### Standing Waves
#### On a String
- two ends of the string are fixed in place:
	- $\lambda = \frac{2L}{n}$ (n = 1, 2, 3. . . )

#### In Open Tubes
- both ends of the tube is open:
	- $\lambda = \frac{2L}{n}$ (n = 1, 2, 3. . . )

#### In Closed Tubes
- one end of the tube is closed, the other is open:
	- $\lambda = \frac{4L}{n}$ (n = 1, 3, 5. . . )

### Thin Film Interference
![[Pasted image 20250325101228.png]]
we label the medium from top to bottom a, b, and c

$\lambda$ in the formulas is the wavelength of light in the thin film, in this case, oil.
- to calculate it, remember **frequency is constant** (it's determined by the light source)
---
- $\Delta x$ is the length of red line minus purple line (path length difference)
- **$\Delta x$ always equals $2t$** (the distance between the surfaces of b and c)

"1" and "2" are reflected light rays (them and the incident ray overlap in reality, the drawing is for demonstration purposes)
- the formulas for constructive and destructive are **exchanged if there's a $\pi$ shift in *one* of the reflected rays**
- how to determine if there's a $\pi$ shift?
	- every time light **reflects off of a slow substance** (the speed of light in which is smaller) there's a $\pi$ shift
	- e.g. in the figure above, both 1 and 2 are $\pi$ shifted, so overall the formulas are not exchanged

## Unlisted Formulas
- half-life: $T_{1/2}$

$m=m_0(\frac{1}{2})^{t/T_{1/2}}$

$N=N_0(\frac{1}{2})^{t/T_{1/2}}$
- probability that one atom will decay within the time interval t (λ is called decay constant)
$P(t)=1−e^{λt}$

## Modern Physics
- work function $\phi$: minimum energy to pull an electron from a metal
- Einstein's photoelectric equation: $E_{photon}=\phi + K_{max}$ ($K_{max}$ and $\phi$ inversely related)
- use above equation with: $E=hf$
- threshold frequency: minimum frequency needed for photoelectric effect
