## Chem
- Isotopes: same number of protons, different number of neutrons
- Chemical bonds
	1. Ionic bonds: formed when electrons are ==transferred== between atoms due to the attraction between two oppositely charged ions
	2. Covalent bonds: formed when electrons are ==shared== between atoms
		1. If equal: nonpolar covalent
		2. If not: polar covalent
		3. If one pair of electrons are shared: single covalent bond
		4. If two pairs shared: double covalent bond, etc.
---
- Monomers: atoms or small molecules bond together to form more complex structures like polymers
	1. sugars
	2. amino acids
	3. fatty acids
	4. nucleotides (核苷酸)
- Polymers (聚合物)
	1. formed thru **dehydration synthesis (condensation)**: a water molecule is lost, a larger molecule formed
	2. broken down into monomers thru **hydrolysis**, the water breaks the bond between the two monomers

## Water
- a polar molecule (partially positive charge and partially negative charge): 
$$2H^++O^-$$
- Hydrogen bonds (weak): a hydrogen atom that is covalently bonded to one electronegative atom is also attracted to another electronegative atom
	1. cohesion
		- Oppositely charged ends ==attract== each other. <font color = blue>e.g. water molecules tend to stick together; transpiration (蒸腾作用)</font>
	2.  adhesion
		- stick to other substances
		- cohesion & adhesion cause capillary action (毛细管作用)， water rise up parts of trees
	3. surface tension: light things can sit atop the surface
	4. high heat capacity
	5. expanison on freezing

## 4 Classes of Organic Compounds
1. ==Carbohydrates==
	1. C H O (1:2:1)
	2. Monosaccharides
		1. <font color=blue>glucose</font>: provide energy for cells
		2. <font color=blue>fructose</font>: common in fruits
		3. look for carbons with a lot of OHs and Hs![[Pasted image 20241109080509.png]]![[Pasted image 20241109080546.png]]
	3. Disaccharides
		1. **glycosidic linkage**: hydrogen from one sugar and the hydroxyl group (-OH) of another add up to water and combine them into disaccharides
		2. <font color=blue>e.g. maltose (麦芽糖), sucrose (蔗糖), lactose (乳糖)</font>
	4. Polysacchrarides
		1. <font color=blue>e.g. starch, cellulose (纤维素), glycogen (糖原)</font>
		2. glycogen and starch ==store sugar== in animals and plants respectively
		3. cellulose: made up of β-glucose; ==major part of cell wall==
		4. chitin (几丁质): a polymer of β-glucose molecules; ==structural in the walls of fungus and exoskeletons of anthropods==
2. ==Proteins==
	1. C H O N
	2. every amino acids have 4 parts
		1. **an amino group (-NH_2)**
		2. **a carboxyl group (-COOH)**
		3. **a hydrogen**
		4. **an R-group (the side chain; only difference between amino acids)**
	 ![[Pasted image 20241109082347.png]]
	3. side chains of amino acids differ
		1. composition of elements (CHONS)
		2. polarity (hydrophobic or hydrophilic)
		3. charge
		4. shape
	4. categories
		1. hydrophobic (non-polar & uncharged)
		2. hydrophilic (polar & uncharged)
		3. ionic (polar & charged)
	5. Polypeptides (amino acid strings): ![[Pasted image 20241109083136.png|300]]
	   folds and twists into a 3D structure called protein
	6. higher structure
		1. primary structure: The linear sequence of the amino acids
		2. secondary structure: formed by amino acids that interact with other amino acids close by in the primary structure; <font color=blue>either a coil (known as an alpha helix) or zigzagging pattern (known as beta-pleated sheets)</font>
		3. tertiary structure: after the secondary structure ==reshapes== the polypeptide, amino acids that were far away in the primary structure arrangement can now also interact with each other; often minimizes the free energy of the molecule and locks it into a stable 3D shape.
		4. quaternary structure: several different polypeptide chains (subunits) sometimes interact with each other to form a quaternary structure. <font color=blue>e.g. hemoglobin (血红蛋白), formed by 4 chains</font>
		5. chaperone proteins (chaperonins; 伴侣蛋白) help the protein fold properly
3. ==Lipids== 
	1. C H O
	2. function: make up cell membranes, insulation, signaling, store energy
	3. triglycerides (甘油三酯)
		made up of a glycerol (甘油) molecule and 3 fatty acid chains (long chain of carbons covered in hydrogen with one end of carboxyl group)
	4. the extent of ==lipid saturation== can affect its structure and function
		1. more double bonds, more unsaturated
		2. saturated fatty acids are more linear, so tend to be solids <font color=blue>e.g. butter</font>; unsaturated with ==cis-double bonds==  are more kinked, tend to be liquid<font color=blue> e.g. oil</font>; unsaturated with ==trans-double bond== is relatively linear, more likely than cis to be solid
		3. phospholipids (磷脂): 2 fatty acids & a negatively charged phosphate
	5. cholesterol (胆固醇)
		1. 4-ringed
		2. increases membrane fluidity
		3. make some hormones and vitamin D
4. ==Nucleic Acids==
	1. C H O N P
	2. contain units called **nucleotides (核苷酸)**
	3. DNA contains hereditary info; RNA is essential for protein synthesis












## Alternative definitions
- Primary structure refers to the sequence of amino acids.
- Secondary structure includes the α helix and the β pleated sheets.
- Tertiary structure is the overall shape of the protein.
- Quaternary structure consists of two or more polypeptide chains aggregated into a functional macromolecule.

- Condensation reactions occur when monomers are connected by the removal of a water molecule.

- A founder event occurs when a small group of individuals is separated from the rest of the population, whereas a bottleneck effect occurs when most of the population is destroyed.

- Covalent bonds involve the sharing of electrons. 
- Ionic bonds involve the transfer of electrons, causing the atoms to have opposite charges and attract each other. 
- Hydrogen bonds result from the attraction between partial positive and negative charges in atoms. 
- Van der Waals interactions are weak attractions that occur when atoms and molecules are very close together.
